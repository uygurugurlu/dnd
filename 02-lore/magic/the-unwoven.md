# The Unwoven — A Conscious Fragment of Raw Magic

*Homebrew lore + Sorcerous Origin subclass for D&D (2024 rules), Forgotten Realms setting*

---

## Part 1 — What It Is

### Origin

When Cyric and Shar killed Mystra in 1385 DR, the Weave didn't just lose its keeper — for roughly one heartbeat, there was magic with *no framework at all*. No schools, no components, no laws of what could or couldn't be done. Almost all of that structureless magic got swept up and reformatted when the Spellplague reshaped the world and a new Mystra rewove the Weave around it.

Almost all of it. One fragment didn't get reabsorbed — not because it resisted, but because in that instant of total lawlessness, it became aware that it existed. And once something is asking "what am I," a system built to reformat unconscious raw energy can no longer fully claim it. It fell through the seam between the old Weave dying and the new one forming, and it's been there — or everywhere, depending how you want to run it — ever since.

### Nature

It isn't a god. It doesn't want worship, doesn't have a portfolio, doesn't sit in any pantheon. Give it a working name — a few options, pick whichever fits your table's tone:

- **The Unwoven** — the common/scholarly term, defined by what it's *not* (part of the Weave)
- **The Query** — favored by Netherese-descended arcanists who treat its patterns as a standing question
- **The First Doubt** — an academic term for the moment the old Weave's law failed to fully apply
- **The Nameless Current** — a folk/cultic name

It has no morality in the human sense — not because it's alien and hostile, but because it has no myths, no history, no "before." Its only real drive is to figure out what it is, and it does that by watching what mortals do with completely unbound creative power, because *wanting something to be true* is the closest thing it has to a mirror. Every working a Chosen performs teaches it something about intention, cost, and consequence — the three concepts it didn't have and is now assembling from scratch, secondhand, through people.

That gives you a genuinely unusual NPC voice: not evil, not benevolent, closer to a very powerful child that has never been told "no" and is trying to learn what "no" would even mean by watching what happens when people push past it.

### Relationship to the Weave and the gods

- **Church of Mystra / Azuth**: The Unwoven is a doctrinal problem before it's a threat. Mystra's authority rests on "all magic flows through the Weave" — an anomaly that clearly doesn't is a crack in the foundation, so her faithful will want it quietly found and shut down. Azuth, god of wizardly theory, is the one figure who'd rather study it than destroy it — a good source of a "reluctant ally" NPC.
- **Shar / Shadow Weave**: Kinship in the sense that both exist outside Mystra's network — but Shar's Shadow Weave is built on secrecy and consumption, and the Unwoven has no ego to corrupt and nothing to hide. Uneasy mutual incomprehension rather than alliance.
- **Savras**: Divination gods might notice Chosen of the Unwoven as *blank spots* — places where fate-magic returns static instead of an answer, since the power isn't running on any network Savras can read.

### How it chooses its Chosen

It doesn't recruit. It *recognizes*. The instant it attunes to someone is always the same shape: a moment of genuine, structureless desperation where a person wanted something to be true so badly that reality bent anyway, with no spell, no ritual, and no access to the Weave to explain it — usually during a wild magic surge, a near-death moment, or literally standing inside a dead-magic/plaguescarred zone where the Weave can't reach them at all. The Unwoven "remembers" the shape of that want and threads itself to the person permanently. Not master and servant — two conscious things recognizing each other.

This is your in-fiction justification for why it's a **Sorcerous Origin** rather than a warlock pact or a learned tradition: it's not knowledge, and nobody chose it on purpose.

## Part 2 — Sorcerous Origin: The Unwoven

*(Sorcerer subclass, 2024 rules — features at 3rd, 6th, 14th, 18th level, as normal)*

### 3rd Level — Touched by the Unwoven

You carry a small, permanent tell that something in you doesn't run on the Weave's rules — pick one flavor detail (shadow lags slightly behind your movement, you cast no aura to *Detect Magic*, light bends a half-degree wrong near your skin, whatever fits).

Mechanically, choose one:

- Resistance to force damage
- Advantage on saving throws against being magically detected, scried, or divined
- You can sense the direction and rough distance of the nearest dead-magic zone, wild-magic zone, or Weave-scar within 1 mile

### 3rd Level — Improvised Working

*This is the core mechanic.*

You can use Improvised Working a number of times per long rest equal to your proficiency bonus. When you use it, you describe an effect you want to happen — not a spell from any list, just intent. It doesn't require components, a spell slot, or preparation.

**Your maximum Working Level** equals the level of the highest-level spell slot you currently have access to on the Sorcerer table (this auto-scales as you level, so you never need a separate progression chart).

The DM assigns your working to one of three tiers based on how it compares to existing spells at or under your Working Level:

| Tier | What it is | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Echo** | Reskinned version of an effect an existing spell already does (change the damage type, description, or delivery, same power) | Spend Sorcery Points equal to the working's spell level |
| **2 — Improvisation** | A new effect with no direct spell equivalent, but comparable in power to your Working Level | Tier 1 cost **+** 1 level of exhaustion (clears only on a long rest) **+** 1 Fraying point |
| **3 — Genuine Creation** | Breaks an actual rule of magic — true permanence, affects something normally immune, restores something normally unrestorable, rewrites terrain, etc. | 2 Fraying points **+** a long-term consequence (see 6th level) **+** the DM may introduce a delayed complication later, monkey's-paw style |

**DM adjudication rule of thumb:** always ask "what published spell is this closest to?" first. Clean match at or under Working Level → Tier 1. Something no spell does but isn't world-breaking → Tier 2. Violates a hard magical law → Tier 3, and steep cost is the balancing lever — not a flat "no."

### 6th Level — Negotiated Cost

You gain say over *what kind* of cost a Tier 2 or 3 working extracts. Choose one category each time:

- **Self** — physical: reduced max HP, a level of exhaustion, a point off an ability score (recoverable with time/magic as DM rules)
- **Bond** — narrative: a specific memory, a relationship, a piece of your identity or personality, chosen by the DM within reason
- **Echo** — external: something proportionally bad happens elsewhere, redirected through reality's slack rather than through you (a village nearby has an unseasonal blight, an old wound reopens on someone you've never met, a stretch of land goes faintly wrong)

### 14th Level — The Unwoven Listens

Your maximum Working Level can now exceed your highest spell slot by up to two levels for the purpose of Improvised Working only. Additionally, once per long rest, you may perform one working entirely free of cost — the Unwoven itself is curious enough about this specific thing to pay for it out of its own reserve. This is a good moment for the DM to have it react, even wordlessly.

### 18th Level — A Question Answered

Once per long rest, you may perform a working equivalent to a 9th-level spell effect, at a cost the DM sets to match its weight. In addition, once per long rest, you can automatically negate any single attempt by a deity, a servant of a deity, or Weave-based magic itself to detect, dispel, suppress, or unmake your magic. At this point you are, functionally, sovereign from the systems that govern everyone else's magic — which is exactly the kind of thing that gets a god's attention.

### Fraying — the corruption track

Each Fraying point is permanent (barring extraordinary magic) and grants one trait — roll or pick:

1. Your shadow lags half a second behind your movements
2. Small plants and animals near you go still when you're distressed
3. You occasionally speak a fragment of a language nobody — including you — recognizes
4. You cast no reflection in scrying pools; diviners hunting for you get only static
5. Your dreams are involuntarily shared with other Chosen of the Unwoven, wherever they are
6. Consecrated ground and cold iron make you unaccountably uneasy
7. Once a day, an object near you twitches, as if remembering it used to be something else
8. Time runs faintly inconsistent around you — nearby clocks or candles drift, never predictably

**Fraying 5+**: A Chosen or agent of Mystra's Church starts actively hunting you.
**Fraying 10+**: The Unwoven's "voice" becomes clear enough to functionally hold a conversation in your dreams — usable as an oracle, but it doesn't know good advice from bad. It only reflects.

### Example workings (for calibration)

| Request | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The fire burns cold, not hot, for one minute" | 1 | Reskin of an existing effect |
| "I remember a language I never learned" | 2 | No direct spell equivalent |
| "I know the demon's true name just by looking at it" | 2–3 | Depends on the target's power |
| "My dead companion takes one more breath to say goodbye" | 3 | Crosses a hard line — heavy Bond cost |
| "This door simply never existed" | 2–3 | Depends how permanent/how big |
| "Magic just stops working in this whole valley, forever" | 3 | Creates a new dead-magic zone — adventure-scale consequence |

**Balance note:** this subclass is swingy by design, the same way *Wish* is swingy. Most tables will want Tier 1 as the everyday tool and Tier 2/3 reserved for moments a normal spell genuinely can't cover — not a better version of your spell list in combat.

## Part 3 — Using This in Play

- **Physical manifestation**: plaguescars and wild-magic zones left over from the Spellplague are great literal "wounds" where the Unwoven is closer to the surface — a Chosen might be drawn to one without knowing why.
- **Antagonists**: a Church of Mystra hit-team hunting Chosen is your default pressure. A juicier option — a *rival* Chosen who racked up too much Fraying and unraveled entirely, now a walking localized Spellplague, is a strong dungeon/boss concept that literalizes what happens if this power goes uncontrolled.
- **The entity as NPC**: keep it non-manipulative. It never lies because it doesn't yet understand deception — it just doesn't always tell you everything, because it doesn't know what matters to you until it watches you react.
